# 🌍 Global Life Expectancy Analysis using WHO Dataset  

## 📖 Overview  
This project analyzes the **Global Life Expectancy Dataset (WHO)** using **R Language**.  
The dataset contains **2,938 records and 22 columns**, covering key health, economic, and social factors that influence life expectancy across different countries and years.  

The primary objective is to uncover **trends, correlations, and variations** in life expectancy worldwide through **data cleaning, statistical analysis, and visualization in R**.

## 📑 Dataset Summary  
- **Records:** 2,938  
- **Columns:** 22  
- **Important Features:**  
  - Life Expectancy  
  - Adult Mortality  
  - Infant Deaths  
  - Alcohol Consumption  
  - GDP  
  - Healthcare Expenditure  
  - Immunization Coverage (Hepatitis B, Polio, etc.)  

📌 Some columns had **missing values** (e.g., *Hepatitis B*, *GDP*), which were handled during preprocessing.  

---

## 📊 Key Analysis Performed in R  
- ✅ **Data Cleaning** – Removed missing values, handled outliers.  
- ✅ **Descriptive Statistics** – Mean, Median, Mode, Standard Deviation.  
- ✅ **Correlation Analysis** – Between life expectancy & key indicators.  
- ✅ **ANOVA Tests** – To compare group means (e.g., regions, income groups).  
- ✅ **Regression Models** – To predict life expectancy from socio-economic and health factors.  
- ✅ **Visualizations** – Histograms, Boxplots, Scatterplots, Heatmaps, Regression plots.  

---

## 📌 Key Findings  
- 📈 Life expectancy shows strong correlation with **GDP and healthcare expenditure**.  
- 🏥 Countries investing more in **healthcare** have higher life expectancy.  
- 👶 High **child & infant mortality rates** drastically lower life expectancy.  
- 🌍 Developed nations consistently outperform developing nations.  
- ♻️ Preventive healthcare (e.g., vaccination, sanitation) significantly boosts longevity.  

---

## 📌 Conclusion  
The **WHO Life Expectancy dataset** highlights how **economic conditions, healthcare infrastructure, and lifestyle factors** influence human longevity worldwide.  

- After cleaning, the dataset became suitable for statistical analysis.  
- Large variations across countries reflect global health inequalities.  
- This analysis shows clear relationships between life expectancy and socio-economic indicators.
